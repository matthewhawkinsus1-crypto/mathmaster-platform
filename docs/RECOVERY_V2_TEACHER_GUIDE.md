# Warm-Up and DOL Recovery — a teacher's guide (Recovery V2)

This guide is for teachers. It explains what Recovery is, what changed, what you
will see, and the one thing you may need to do for an assignment students have
already started. No programming knowledge is needed.

## What Recovery is

When a Warm-Up or a DOL closes, a student who did not do well gets a **second
try** — a *Recovery* — made of **fresh questions on the same skills**, at the
same difficulty, in the same tool, graded by MathMaster.

- It is offered only **after the original closes**. While the Warm-Up or DOL
  is still open, students never see it, so it is never an easier way out.
- A student **unlocks** it by showing they have learned the skill: by default,
  **7 of their last 8 practice questions right, on their own** (no hints, no
  worked examples, no teacher help).
- It is open until the assignment's **final submission date**.
- It **can only raise a grade**. The recorded score is the higher of the
  original and the Recovery, and a Recovery counts up to **85%** for a Warm-Up
  and **90%** for a DOL (an excused make-up can earn full credit).
- There is no second gradebook and nothing extra to grade: the Recovery result
  goes into the same Warm-Up or DOL grade, the same Grade Export, and the same
  Google Classroom grade.

## What changed in Recovery V2

1. **Every new lesson starts recoverable.** When you create an assignment,
   Pre-Flight pairs each Warm-Up and DOL question with a checked *Question
   Family* — a generator that writes fresh versions of that kind of question —
   and stores the pairing with the assignment. You never write Recovery
   questions or set up a generator yourself. Your questions are not changed.
2. **The assignment's own Practice now counts.** A Practice question in the
   assignment counts toward unlocking Recovery when it practises the same
   skill (the same TEKS) as the Warm-Up or DOL, MathMaster graded the
   student's own answer, and the student answered it **without help**. The
   student is told exactly which Practice questions count ("Practice Q1–Q13 in
   this assignment count when you answer them without a hint or help").
3. **Assignments students already started can get Recovery too** — without
   changing a single question or a single student's work (see "Prepare
   Recovery" below).

## In Pre-Flight: "Warm-Up and DOL Recovery"

The Check step shows a panel for the Warm-Up and the DOL (and, for an Honors
copy, a second panel for the Honors version):

| What it says | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Ready** | Students who need it will be offered a fresh Recovery. Each question shows where its Recovery comes from. |
| **Not ready** | At least one question cannot be recovered yet. The reason is under the question. |
| **Turned off** | Recovery is switched off for this assignment. |
| **Live Challenge — the result is the Warm-Up grade** | The Warm-Up is played as a Live Challenge; the game result is the grade, so there is no Warm-Up Recovery. |
| **Uses its own retest process** | A secure test or Test Cycle: it has its own retests. |

At the top, one sentence tells you whether the whole assignment is
Recovery-ready. It only says "Ready" when **both** the Warm-Up and the DOL are
ready (or use one of the exceptions above).

**"Fix for Recovery."** Under a question that is not ready, this button asks
MathMaster AI (or an outside AI, by copy and paste) for a version of that
question that keeps the same skill, TEKS, difficulty and tool *and* can be
recovered. MathMaster only accepts the replacement if it passes every
Pre-Flight check **and** a Recovery can then be prepared for it; otherwise
nothing changes and it tells you why.

A Warm-Up is ready when at least one of its questions is ready (the Recovery
draws its fresh questions from those). A DOL needs every question ready.

## For an assignment students already started: "Prepare Recovery"

Assignments created before Recovery V2 may have Warm-Up and DOL questions with
no Recovery. When MathMaster can add Recovery to one of them, its card on the
Assignments page shows **RECOVERY CAN BE ADDED**, and its ⋮ menu has
**Prepare Recovery…**. Both open the assignment details, where the
**Warm-Up & DOL Recovery** section shows:

- readiness **as students have it right now** (a question whose Recovery has
  not been approved yet reads "not ready yet");
- the Recovery version, once one is saved;
- a **Prepare Recovery…** button.

Prepare Recovery shows a preview before anything is saved:

- **What this adds** — for each Warm-Up and DOL question, where its fresh
  versions come from, how many there are, and that they keep the same TEKS,
  DOK and tool.
- **Recovery readiness** — each section before and after you approve.
- **Still not ready** — any question that still cannot be recovered, and why.
- **What stays exactly the same** — every question, every student's answers,
  attempts, scores, saved drafts and Recovery records, the due dates, the
  classes, the Google Classroom posts and gradebook columns. Approving does not
  send anything to Classroom, because no grade changes.

Press **Approve and Add Recovery**. MathMaster checks again that nothing
changed since your preview; if a question changed in the meantime, it changes
nothing and asks you to look again. Approving twice does nothing the second
time.

### The live Algebra I lesson

"**Algebra I — Vertical and Horizontal Transformations of Linear Functions**"
was assigned before Recovery V2. Its three Warm-Up and three DOL questions had
no Recovery. They are now paired with five new Question Families, reviewed
question by question:

| Question | Recovery versions |
| --- | --- |
| Warm-Up Q1 — graph g(x) = 3x − 8 | graph a line from slope-intercept form, whole-number slope |
| Warm-Up Q2 — key characteristics of g(x) = 3x − 8 | key characteristics of a line, whole-number slope, fractional x-intercept |
| Warm-Up Q3 — key characteristics of h(x) = −(2/3)x + 5 | key characteristics of a line, fractional slope and x-intercept |
| DOL Q1 — plot the images of S1–S3 for g(x) = f(x + 2) | plot the images of f(x) = x under a horizontal shift |
| DOL Q2 — j(x) = 3x + 1, r(x) = j(x − 2) | a horizontal shift of a linear function, written two ways |
| DOL Q3 — horizontal vs. vertical transformations of f(x) = x | compare the effect of a horizontal and a vertical transformation |

Every Recovery version is a new problem (never one already in the lesson),
graded by MathMaster, in the same tool, at the same DOK and difficulty.

**What you need to do:** open the assignment's details (for the Standard copy,
and again for the Honors copy if you have one), press **Prepare Recovery…**,
read the preview, and press **Approve and Add Recovery**. Nothing changes for
any student until you do.

### The Algebra II Honors interval lessons

"**Algebra II Honors — Lesson 1 Day 1: Interval Notation, Domain, and Range**"
and "**Algebra II Honors — Lesson 1 Day 2: Function Attributes and Relations**"
(Module 1) ask students to graph an inequality on the number line and write it
in interval notation. Those number-line questions can now be recovered:

| Question | Recovery versions |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 Warm-Up Q1 — graph −4 ≤ x < 3 | graph a bounded interval and write it in interval notation |
| Day 1 Warm-Up Q2 — graph x > 2 | graph a ray and write it in interval notation |
| Day 1 Warm-Up Q3 — graph x ≤ −5 or x > 1 | graph two rays and write the union |
| Day 1 DOL Q1 — graph x < −1 or 2 ≤ x ≤ 5 | graph a ray and a bounded interval and write the union |
| Day 2 Warm-Up Q2 — graph −2 ≤ x < 4 | graph a bounded interval and write it in interval notation |

Each version states a new inequality with whole-number ends, in the same tool,
at the same DOK and difficulty. Prepare Recovery makes both Warm-Ups ready. The
DOLs stay "not ready": Day 1's DOL Q2 and Q3 and Day 2's DOL have questions no
Recovery family writes yet.

## Seeing who can recover

In the assignment details, **Show who can recover in (class)** lists, for the
Warm-Up and the DOL:

| Group | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Held for your review | MathMaster could not grade a Recovery question. Open the student's work in Grades to decide. |
| Cannot be offered | Recovery is not ready for this assignment, or it is turned off. |
| Practicing to unlock | The original closed; the line under the name says how close they are ("5 of the last 8 right on their own; needs 7"). |
| Unlocked | They may start the second try. |
| In progress | They started it. |
| Completed | Finished; the higher score is recorded. |
| Not needed | The original is already at or above what a Recovery can record. |
| Original still open | The Warm-Up or DOL has not closed yet. |
| Closed | The final submission date passed. |

Each student's Recovery history, with the practice that unlocked it, is in
their work in Grades, as before.

## What students see

On Home, in the Assignments list and on the assignment's results page, a
student whose Warm-Up or DOL closed below the cap sees a Recovery card with:

- when the original closed;
- the bar ("Get 7 of your last 8 practice questions right on your own");
- **exactly which practice counts** — the "Practice for …" questions, and the
  assignment's own Practice questions on the same skills, by number — or, if
  the assignment's Practice is on other skills, that it does not count;
- a progress meter and how many of their recent right answers came from each
  kind of practice;
- the date the Recovery closes;
- after finishing: their original, Recovery and final score.

## Exceptions

- A **Live Challenge** Warm-Up: the game result is the Warm-Up grade, so there
  is no Warm-Up Recovery.
- A **secure test or Test Cycle**: it uses its own retest process.
- **Recovery turned off** for an assignment: nothing is offered.
- **No grade is ever invented.** Preparing Recovery creates no score for
  anyone. A student who missed the Warm-Up or DOL earns a score only by doing
  the work: unlocking and completing the Recovery (counted up to its cap). A
  student who never joined a Live Challenge Warm-Up has no result, as before.

## Questions teachers ask

**Will any student lose work or a grade?** No. Preparing Recovery writes one
thing beside the questions and never touches a student's answers, attempts,
scores or drafts. A Recovery can only raise a grade.

**Will the questions my students already answered change?** No. They keep their
wording, numbers, answers and tools. The Recovery versions are stored next to
them, not in them.

**Does Google Classroom get a new post or a grade change?** Preparing Recovery
does neither. When a student *completes* a Recovery, their improved grade goes
to Classroom and to Grade Export exactly the way any grade change does.

**What if a question says "not ready"?** Students are not offered a Recovery
that MathMaster cannot make well. For a new lesson, use **Fix for Recovery** in
Pre-Flight. For an assignment students have already answered, the question is
kept as it is; a future copy of the lesson can be fixed in Pre-Flight.
